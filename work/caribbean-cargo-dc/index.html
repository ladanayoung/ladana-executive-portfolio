01 / Context

A business built on complex service journeys.

Caribbean Cargo DC (CCDC) is a licensed Mid-Atlantic freight-forwarding and export logistics company serving individual, institutional, and commercial customers with international shipping needs. Service involves documentation, shipment options, coordination, and contact across multiple stakeholders.

In a high-consideration, operationally complex environment, communication must build confidence while remaining consistent with what frontline teams can deliver.

02 / Problem

Reduce ambiguity without oversimplifying the business.

  • Information friction. Recurring questions about shipping services, documentation, and processes can create repetitive explanations and customer confusion.
  • Premium differentiation. The value of a White Glove offering needs to be communicated as benefits customers understand, not just as operational jargon.
  • Multiple audiences. Executive leadership, office teams, customers, embassies, international organizations, and destination partners require clear and credible communications.

03 / Approach

Listen → map → design → align.

Rather than treat communications as a stream of isolated deliverables, the work connected audience needs, service explanations, and stakeholder relationships.

01

Translate customer questions into usable information.

Collaborated with office personnel on common questions and customer needs. Used customer personas and audience-centered thinking to structure a navigable FAQ concept in Figma.

02

Design a clearer self-service experience.

Organized service content into discoverable questions and answers, with expandable content and navigational cues to help customers find relevant information.

03

Position White Glove services around customer value.

Developed messaging that communicates premium service through reliability, individualized attention, and clarity about what customers receive.

04

Coordinate communications across functions and borders.

Supported executive, office operations, embassy, international organization, and destination-agent relationships to improve message consistency and service understanding.

Process lens: Lean Six Sigma Yellow and White Belt training reinforced a continuous-improvement approach to finding recurring friction and making information more consistent. No formal DMAIC implementation or measured processing-time reduction is claimed.

04 / Results & scope

Business-relevant deliverables, not vanity metrics.

  • Customer-information design: an audience-informed FAQ concept and content architecture for recurring service questions.
  • Premium-service positioning: a differentiated messaging foundation for the White Glove offering.
  • Cross-stakeholder support: sustained communications involving executives, office operations, embassies, international organizations, and destination agents.

Growth context: the company reportedly grew from under $1 million to more than $2 million in revenue over the broader engagement period. This is company-level growth, and this case study does not claim communications caused or independently measured that increase.

05 / Leadership takeaway

Communications can function as operational infrastructure.

In complex businesses, the value of strategic communications is not confined to a campaign launch. It also shows up in clearer customer journeys, more consistent executive narratives, and the discipline required to coordinate trust across functions and markets.

What this demonstrates: user-centered thinking, stakeholder judgment, cross-functional partnership, and the ability to translate complicated business processes into understandable experiences.
